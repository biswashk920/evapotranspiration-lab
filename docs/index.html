How it works

Penman-Monteith (FAO-56), the reference method:

ET₀ = [0.408 Δ (Rn − G) + γ · 900/(T+273) · u₂ (eₛ − eₐ)] / [Δ + γ (1 + 0.34 u₂)]

Δ slope of the vapour pressure curve, Rn net radiation, G soil heat flux (taken as 0 for a day), γ psychrometric constant, u₂ wind at 2 m, eₛ − eₐ vapour pressure deficit. It needs temperature, humidity, wind and sunshine or radiation.

Hargreaves-Samani:

ET₀ = 0.0023 (Tmean + 17.8) √(Tmax − Tmin) Ra

Temperature and latitude only. It tends to overestimate in humid, cloudy places and underestimate where wind is strong.

Thornthwaite:

PET = 16 (10 T / I)a × daylight/12 × days/30

Monthly mean temperature only, built from US conditions. It often underestimates in dry climates and in the tropics.

Other methods in the comparison. PM, temperature only follows FAO-56 for stations without humidity or radiation data: Rs = 0.16 √(Tmax − Tmin) Ra, dew point = Tmin − 2 °C, wind = 2 m/s. Priestley-Taylor: ET₀ = 1.26 Δ/(Δ+γ) · Rn/λ, which drops the wind and humidity term. Blaney-Criddle (simple form): ET₀ = p (0.46 T + 8.13), with p the day's share of annual daylight hours. Calibrated Hargreaves multiplies Hargreaves by a least-squares factor k fitted to Penman-Monteith.

Aridity index = annual rainfall ÷ annual ET₀ (UNEP classes: below 0.05 hyper-arid, to 0.2 arid, to 0.5 semi-arid, to 0.65 dry sub-humid, above that humid). The warming slider raises temperature only; humidity, wind and sunshine stay fixed.
